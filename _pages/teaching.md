<div class="teaching-intro">
  <p class="eyebrow">From equations to evidence</p>
  <p class="teaching-lede">Across mechanics, laboratory physics, molecular biophysics, and cancer biology, students learn to move between physical models, computation, data, and scientific argument.</p>
  <p>Python, simulation, machine learning, and generative AI enter the classroom where they sharpen a question or expose an assumption. The standard remains the same: students must understand the physics, test the output, and communicate what the evidence supports.</p>
</div>

<div class="teaching-pathway" aria-label="Teaching approach">
  <div><span>01</span><strong>Model</strong><p>Translate a physical or biological question into assumptions, variables, and equations.</p></div>
  <div><span>02</span><strong>Compute</strong><p>Use Python, numerical methods, simulation, or AI to explore what the model predicts.</p></div>
  <div><span>03</span><strong>Test</strong><p>Compare results with data, limiting cases, uncertainty, and established scientific knowledge.</p></div>
  <div><span>04</span><strong>Communicate</strong><p>Explain the method, evidence, limitations, and next question clearly.</p></div>
</div>

## Current and upcoming courses

<p class="section-intro">The courses below reflect active and upcoming teaching. Assignments change by term; the <a href="https://bulletin.wfu.edu/courses-az/phy/">undergraduate</a> and <a href="https://bulletin.wfu.edu/graduate/courses-az/phy/">graduate Physics bulletins</a> remain the authoritative sources for course descriptions.</p>

<div class="course-grid">
  <article class="course-card">
    <div class="course-card__meta"><span class="course-code">FYS 100</span><span>First-year seminar · Fall 2026</span></div>
    <h3>AI and the Physics of Life: AlphaFold and Why Molecular Shape Matters</h3>
    <p>Students use protein structure prediction as a route into the relationship between molecular shape and biological function. AlphaFold becomes both a scientific tool and a case study in what an AI prediction can—and cannot—explain.</p>
    <ul class="technique-list" aria-label="Techniques"><li>AlphaFold</li><li>molecular structure</li><li>AI evaluation</li></ul>
  </article>

  <article class="course-card">
    <div class="course-card__meta"><span class="course-code">PHY 262</span><span>Undergraduate physics</span></div>
    <h3>Mechanics</h3>
    <p>Students turn equations of motion into computational models, use Python to study systems that resist simple solutions, and visualize how assumptions shape predictions. A generative-AI exercise asks students to reproduce a difficult modeling task, diagnose the output, and explain the physics needed to judge it.</p>
    <ul class="technique-list" aria-label="Techniques"><li>Python</li><li>numerical modeling</li><li>visualization</li><li>AI critique</li></ul>
  </article>

  <article class="course-card">
    <div class="course-card__meta"><span class="course-code">PHY 266</span><span>Team-taught laboratory</span></div>
    <h3>Intermediate Laboratory II</h3>
    <p>The course connects experiments in mechanics with computer simulation. Its computational work emphasizes Python, data analysis, and machine learning, asking students to move carefully between measured data, models, uncertainty, and scientific presentation.</p>
    <ul class="technique-list" aria-label="Techniques"><li>experiments</li><li>Python</li><li>data analysis</li><li>machine learning</li></ul>
  </article>

  <article class="course-card">
    <div class="course-card__meta"><span class="course-code">PHY 361/661</span><span>Undergraduate and graduate seminar</span></div>
    <h3>Generative AI in Biophysics</h3>
    <p>Students read, present, and discuss current work in AI, deep learning, molecular biophysics, and drug discovery. Source-grounded research tools support literature exploration, while presentations and reflection keep scientific judgment and responsible research practice at the center.</p>
    <ul class="technique-list" aria-label="Techniques"><li>primary literature</li><li>deep learning</li><li>drug discovery</li><li>research reflection</li></ul>

## Mentoring from coursework into research

<div class="feature-grid">
  <article class="feature-card">
    <h3>Increasing independence</h3>
    <p>Students begin by learning a physical system and a computational workflow, then take ownership of the question, validation, interpretation, and communication.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="feature-card">
    <h3>Research-ready habits</h3>
    <p>Reproducible analysis, careful figures, critical reading, and constructive discussion are treated as scientific practices—not finishing touches.</p>
  </article>
</div>

## AI as a tool—and a subject of study

Generative AI is useful when it supports learning rather than bypassing it. Students compare generated answers with equations, data, and primary sources; identify failure modes; and remain responsible for the scientific claims they make.

Read the Wake Forest Center for the Advancement of Teaching profile, [“Fred Salsbury: AI as a Tool, Not a Shortcut”](https://cat.wfu.edu/resources/ai/wfuexperience/fred-salsbury/).
