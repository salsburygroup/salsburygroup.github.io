## Mentoring and collaboration

<div class="feature-grid">
  <article class="feature-card">
    <p class="eyebrow">Students</p>
    <h3>Develop research independence</h3>
    <p>Students learn a physical system and computational workflow, then take increasing ownership of validation, interpretation, reproducibility, and scientific communication.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="feature-card">
    <p class="eyebrow">Collaborators</p>
    <h3>Connect computation with experiment</h3>
    <p>Collaborative projects begin with a well-defined biological or molecular question and a realistic plan for how simulation or data analysis can provide testable insight.</p>

## Opportunities {#opportunities}

The current Wake Forest Physics biophysics directory lists the Salsbury Group as accepting graduate and undergraduate students. Availability can change with project fit, preparation, timing, and funding, so prospective researchers should confirm current opportunities before making plans.

<div class="feature-grid">
  <article class="feature-card">

    <h3>Apply through Wake Forest Physics</h3>
    <p>Review the official graduate-program requirements and application process, then contact the group with your scientific interests and relevant preparation.</p>
  </article>
  <article class="feature-card">
